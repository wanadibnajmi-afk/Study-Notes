Topic 7: Flywheel Formula Sheet & Exam Guide

Complete reference from Dr. Mohd Syahril Ramadhan Bin Mohd Saufi's lecture slides (SEMM2223).

1. Speed Fluctuation

Coefficient of Fluctuation of Speed (α)

α = (ωmax − ωmin) / ωmean
α = (Nmax − Nmin) / Nmean
ωmean = (ωmax + ωmin) / 2 = 2πNmean / 60

Measures allowable speed variation relative to the mean operating speed.

2. Energy Fluctuation

Maximum Fluctuation of Energy (ΔE = βE)

ΔE = βE = Emax − Emin
ΔE = ½ I (ωmax² − ωmin²)
ΔE = βE = I · α · ωmean²

Work done per cycle: E = Tmean · θcycle, and β = ΔE / E.

3. Power & Inertia

Power & Moment of Inertia

Power: P = Tmean · ωmean (Watts)
Inertia: I = (βE) / (α · ωmean²)
Disc: I = ½ m r²  |  Rim: I = m k²

For solid cylinder/disc: m = ρ · V = ρ · (π r² t).

Type 1: Variable Input → Constant Output

Examples: Internal Combustion (IC) Engines, Steam Engines.

  • Above Tmean (+Area): Input torque exceeds load torque (Tin > Tmean). Crankshaft accelerates; flywheel absorbs energy.
  • Below Tmean (−Area): Input torque is less than load (Tin < Tmean). Crankshaft decelerates; flywheel releases energy.

Type 2: Constant Input → Variable Output

Examples: Punching Machines, Shearing Machines, Rolling Mills.

  • Below Tmean (+Area for Flywheel): During idle stroke (Tload < Tmotor), motor energy goes into speeding up the flywheel (absorbs energy).
  • Above Tmean (−Area for Flywheel): During punching stroke (Tload > Tmotor), heavy load slows the flywheel down (releases energy).

Common Exam Traps to Watch Out For!

Trap 1: "±" Speed Fluctuation
If a question says "speed within ±0.75% of mean speed" (Slide 29), the total fluctuation is α = 0.75% + 0.75% = 1.5% = 0.015!
Trap 2: Time (t) vs Angle (θ)
Work done is ∫ T dθ, NOT ∫ T dt! If the x-axis is in seconds (t), always multiply widths by ωmean (θ = ωt) before computing energy areas in Joules!
Trap 3: Total System Inertia
If the motor or machine parts already have inertia Imotor (Slide 34), subtract it from Itotal to get Iflywheel = Itotal − Imotor before finding flywheel dimensions!
