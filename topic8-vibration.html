Understanding Mechanical Vibration

Vibration is any cyclical "back-and-forth" oscillation of a mechanical structure around an equilibrium position. To analyze it, we model physical machines using three fundamental elements: Mass (m) which stores kinetic energy, Spring (k) which stores potential energy, and Damper (c) which dissipates energy as heat.

Part A: Free Undamped vs. Free Damped vs. Forced Vibration

C = 0, F = 0 ζ = 0

1. Free Undamped Vibration

The system is displaced or struck once and allowed to oscillate freely with no friction or damping. Total mechanical energy (T + U) stays constant forever.

Equation of Motion:
m·ẍ + k·x = 0
Response:
x(t) = X0 cos(ωnt)
Key Trait: Oscillates perpetually at constant amplitude X0 and natural frequency ωn = √(k/m).
C > 0, F = 0 ζ > 0

2. Free Damped Vibration

Disturbed once with no continuous external force, but a viscous damper (c) removes energy. Vibrations gradually decay until the mass rests at equilibrium.

Equation of Motion:
m·ẍ + c·ẋ + k·x = 0
Underdamped Response (ζ < 1):
x(t) = X·e-ζωnt sin(ωdt + φ)
Key Trait: Oscillates slower than undamped (ωd = ωn√(1-ζ²)) with exponentially shrinking peaks.
F(t) ≠ 0 ωf

3. Forced Vibration

An external periodic force F(t) = F0sin(ωft) is repeatedly applied (e.g., rotating unbalance or bumpy road), sustaining the vibration continuously.

Equation of Motion:
m·ẍ + c·ẋ + k·x = F(t)
Resonance Danger:
Occurs when ωf ≈ ωn
Key Trait: Steady-state vibration happens at the forcing frequency ωf, not ωn.

Part B: The Three Damping Cases (Damping Ratio ζ = c / cc)

Critical damping cc = 2√(km) = 2mωn is the exact threshold that prevents oscillation.

Underdamped (ζ < 1) Oscillates

Damping is less than critical (c < cc). The mass overshoots equilibrium and oscillates back and forth with exponentially decaying amplitude. Has damped frequency ωd.

Critically Damped (ζ = 1) Fastest Stop

Damping equals critical (c = cc). The system returns to equilibrium in the shortest possible time without overshooting. Ideal for door closers and precision gauges.

Overdamped (ζ > 1) Sluggish

Damping exceeds critical (c > cc). Heavy resistance prevents any oscillation, causing the mass to creep slowly back to equilibrium.

Part C: 1DOF vs. 2DOF Systems at a Glance

Degrees of Freedom (DOF) = minimum number of independent coordinates needed to specify the position of all masses at any time.

Characteristic Single Degree of Freedom (1DOF) Two Degrees of Freedom (2DOF)
Independent Coordinates 1 coordinate (e.g., x or θ) 2 coordinates (e.g., x1, x2 or x, θ)
Equation of Motion Meqẍ + Ceqẋ + Keqx = 0 [M]{ẍ} + [C]{ẋ} + [K]{x} = {0} (2 coupled ODEs)
Natural Frequencies 1 natural frequency (ωn) 2 natural frequencies (ωn1 and ωn2)
Mode Shapes None (only 1 mass moves) 2 Mode Shapes: Mode 1 (In-phase) & Mode 2 (Out-of-phase)
Engineering Application Simple machine mount, quarter-car suspension Half-car bounce & pitch, Dynamic Vibration Absorber

1DOF Vibration Formula Sheet

Natural Frequency (ωn) ωn = √(Keq / Meq) = √(g / δst)
Frequency (Hz) & Period (s) fn = ωn / 2π ,   Tn = 2π / ωn
Critical Damping (Cc) Cc = 2√(KeqMeq) = 2Meqωn
Damping Ratio (ζ) ζ = Ceq / Cc = Ceq / (2Meqωn)
Damped Frequency (ωd) ωd = ωn√(1 - ζ²) ,   Td = 2π / ωd
Logarithmic Decrement (n cycles) ln(X0 / Xn) = (n · 2πζ) / √(1 - ζ²)

Equivalent Elements & 2DOF Equations

Parallel Springs / Dampers Keq = K1 + K2 ,   Ceq = C1 + C2
Series Springs / Dampers 1/Keq = 1/K1 + 1/K2
Homogeneous Rod (Pinned End) IO = mL²/3 ⇒ Meq,tip = m / 3
Uniform Pulley Disc (Rim) IO = mr²/2 ⇒ Meq,rim = m / 2
Simple Pendulum Keq = mg / L ⇒ ωn = √(g / L)
